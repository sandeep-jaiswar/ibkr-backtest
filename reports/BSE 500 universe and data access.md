# A BSE 500 universe is buildable, but IBKR access is unproven

We can build a BSE 500 universe file today: a 500-name snapshot with BSE scrip code, ISIN and NSE symbol, joined through ISIN to NSE's live master file (498 of 500 match). It is a current-membership snapshot from a third-party page, not a point-in-time history, so it carries survivorship bias from day one. Whether we can get BSE daily bars from Interactive Brokers is **unverified, and the evidence we have points against it**. One undated IBKR knowledge-base snippet says IBKR does not offer BSE trading, and the IBKR pages that would settle the question returned HTTP 403. The blocker is therefore a single account check: search for a BSE contract in TWS or Client Portal and request one year of daily bars. If that fails, the BSE work needs a different price source, and that choice should be made before any BSE backtest is built. Cash-equity modelling is well understood and costs about **22 bps per delivery round trip** before slippage. The corporate-action and price-band rules matter more than the fee arithmetic, because raw exchange bars show false breakouts at every bonus ex-date.

## The universe can be built, but only as a survivor snapshot

The working list is a Dhan capture of 500 rows with 500 unique BSE scrip codes and ISINs, retrieved 2026-11-17 from [Dhan BSE 500 page](https://dhan.co/indices/bse-500-sector-weightage). Joining it to [NSE's EQUITY_L.csv](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/equities/EQUITY_L.csv) by ISIN gives an NSE symbol for **498 of 500** names, and every matched symbol agrees with the snapshot. The two misses are National Stock Exchange of India (ISIN INE721I01024) and National Securities Depository (INE301O01023). NSE's row looks suspicious and needs checking against BSE before it enters the universe. ISIN, not symbol, is the reliable join key.

The constituent list itself is weaker than the join. BSE's own archive pages returned a JavaScript shell or HTTP 403 to scripted access, so the official BSE constituent file was never downloaded. Investing.com shows **488** members for the same index ([Investing.com](https://uk.investing.com/indices/s-p-bse-500-components)), so the source counts disagree and cannot be reconciled from here. The index is reviewed semi-annually in June and December, and a methodology change effective **22 December 2025** moved the eligible pool from BSE AllCap to BSE 1000 and ranked stocks by traded value and free-float market cap ([rhnvrm circular mirror](https://rhnvrm.github.io/stock-market-circulars/circulars/bse/2025/bse-2025-08-01-70954565938a712e-change-in-methodology-to-the-bse-indices), secondary). The 2026 changes, including the Vedanta demerger reconstitution, mean that names in older lists may no longer exist ([multibagg note](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/bse-indices-rejig-june-2026-cmph0932q8qkytg0j656l81ob)).

For a backtest that starts before 2026, the snapshot is wrong by construction. No public point-in-time BSE 500 archive was found. The route is to rebuild membership from BSE reconstitution notices, such as the [June 2024 notice](https://bseindia.com/downloads/SPDJ_ANN/MediaReleasePDF/Reconstitution_of_BSE_Indices_June_2024.pdf), and third-party circular archives. Coverage for 2015, 2018 and 2021 was not checked, and no delisted-company list was found. Until that history exists, any BSE result is a survivor result and should be labelled as such.

## IBKR access to BSE bars is unverified and may not exist

The IBKR evidence is thin. The official pages on contracts, India exchanges, market-data pricing and India fees all returned 403, so the claims below rest on search snippets, a mirror of the TWS API docs, and secondary sources. The strongest signal is negative. An undated IBKR knowledge-base snippet says BSE trading is not offered, while the only Indian exchange seen in IBKR market-data material is NSE, with a 15-minute-delayed entry and a separate real-time "Capital Market Segment" subscription. A 2009 press release says NSE stock trading was added for residents and NRIs ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/28410/interactive-brokers-adds-indian-stock-trading)), but an undated FAQ says stock trading is not yet available. The current status is **unknown**. Exchange code, primary exchange, conId, historical depth and subscription fees for BSE are all unknown.

What we did verify is the API pacing and depth regime, from the [IBKR TWS historical limitations mirror](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/historical_limitations.html). Bars of 30 seconds or less older than six months are unavailable. Daily bars with a one-year duration fall within the allowed step sizes. Identical requests must not repeat within 15 seconds, and small-bar requests are limited to 60 per ten minutes. The daily-bar pacing is not a constraint for a 500-name universe, but the depth for Indian daily bars has not been tested. Residency also matters: IBKR treats anyone living in India more than 182 days a year as a resident, and NRI accounts need an Indian bank account such as an NRO account (search snippets from IBKR India pages, not fetched). Whether the project's account has India trading permission is unknown.

The corporate-action behaviour of IBKR bars is also only partly known. The quoted IBKR specification says TRADES bars are split-adjusted but not dividend-adjusted ([QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/9252/inconsistency-between-backtest-historical-data-and-and-live-historical-data-over-stock-split-events-for-raw-datanormalizationmode/)). Bonus-issue adjustment is documented nowhere we found, and community reports conflict on dividend capture. We should not rely on IBKR adjustments without reconciling them to BSE corporate-action records.

## Indian cash costs and settlement must be explicit in the model

A BSE delivery round trip costs about **0.224%** in statutory charges. The buy side is STT 0.10%, stamp duty 0.015%, BSE exchange charge 0.00375%, SEBI fee 0.0001% and GST on the exchange and SEBI charges. The sell side is STT 0.10%, exchange charge, SEBI fee and GST. The BSE exchange rate of 0.00375% is consistent across [Zerodha's schedule](https://zerodha.com/charges), HDFC Sky and Tradejini. Delivery brokerage at Zerodha is zero, but a ₹15.34 DP charge applies per scrip on each delivery sell. IBKR's own India commission for BSE was not found. A secondary Finextra figure of 5 bps with a ₹75 minimum is undated and should not be used until confirmed. Stamp duty is 0.015% in current broker pages, against an older 0.01% figure. The effect of that choice on the round trip is about 0.005 percentage points.

Holding period decides the cost regime. A next-day exit is a delivery trade on both legs, and the sale settles T+1. A same-day round trip is intraday, at roughly **0.037%** statutory plus brokerage, because the STT rate is 0.025% on the sell side and stamp duty is 0.003% on the buy side. The backtest must classify each trade, because using intraday rates for a next-day exit understates costs by about six times. Cash settlement is T+1, and [optional T+0 is limited to Qualified Stock Brokers](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2025/1761825190101.pdf) with a deadline that was extended again without a new date. A conservative model blocks reuse of sale proceeds until the next trading day. Whether brokers permit same-day reuse was not verified.

Price bands are the modelling issue that changes fills. Securities without derivatives have fixed bands, a 20% maximum, which BSE and the exchanges cut to 10% or 5% through surveillance actions ([BSE surveillance page](https://bseindia.com/static/markets/equity/EQReports/sur_Surveillance.html)). Aggregator reports describe 2026 revisions that cut some scrips to 1%, 2% or 5% ([rhnvrm tag page](https://rhnvrm.github.io/stock-market-circulars/tags/price-band-revision/), secondary). A stock at its lower limit cannot be sold, so a stop below the limit should fill at the next tradable price with the locked day recorded. Band treatment for F&O-listed BSE 500 names was not found, and no historical per-scrip band dataset exists in our sources. Tick size is ₹0.01 for the lowest band, but the threshold where ₹0.05 begins is disputed between ₹100 and ₹250. Keep it as a parameter. Lot size is one share, and fractional holdings are not available, so position sizing must round down to whole shares.

Corporate actions are the largest data risk. Exchange series are raw, so a bonus or split shows up as a price discontinuity on the ex-date. The SEBI education deck gives the bonus factor as (A+B)/B ([SEBI deck](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/pdf/reference-material/ppt/PPT-5%20Corporate%20Actions%20-%20Dividends,%20Bonus,%20Splits,%20etc_.pdf)). That makes a 1:1 bonus a -50% price move on the ex-date and a 2:1 bonus -66.7%. BSE's own 2:1 bonus in May 2025 was applied this way ([MSEI circular 17190](https://www.msei.in/SX-Content/Circulars/2025/May/Circular-17190.pdf)). Any unadjusted series will therefore show false breakouts and false stop-outs. Rights issues have no formula in the sources we found, so they stay a known gap. One practitioner reported that yfinance mishandled the HDFCBANK demerger and distorted two years of backtest returns ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/has-anyone-found-wrong-adjusted-prices-in-nse-historical-data-for-backtesting/192938)). Every data source needs a spot-check against known events before use.

## Conclusion

The decision turns on one check, and it can be done in minutes in TWS. Search for a BSE stock such as Reliance (scrip 500325), record the exchange, primaryExchange, currency and conId, and request one year of daily bars. If the contract is missing, or the bars do not come back, IBKR cannot be the BSE data source, and the choice becomes a separate vendor or exchange-file pipeline. That pipeline is more work, because it needs raw bhavcopy history plus a corporate-action file, but it is the only route that handles the bonus and dividend issues we cannot verify through IBKR. If the bars do come back, the next test is the bonus-issue reconciliation against BSE's corporate-action record for at least one event, because the IBKR adjustment policy is undocumented.

Whatever the data path, the first backtest must be labelled survivor-biased until point-in-time membership exists. The current 500-name file is a sound starting universe for a forward-looking test, not for a historical one. The next concrete steps are: run the TWS contract check; export the Dhan table into a universe CSV with ISIN as the key and a source-date column; check the NSE row; and fetch the June 2024 and later reconstitution notices to start the membership history. The universe CSV was not produced in this run. The report covers the decision, and the file can be generated mechanically from the constituent table in the research notes once the data path is chosen.
